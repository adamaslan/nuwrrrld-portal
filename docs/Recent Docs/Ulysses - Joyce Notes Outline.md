## II. Structure: The 18 Episodes

### Part I — The Telemachiad (1–3)
*Stephen's morning: guilt, poverty, exile*

1. **Telemachus** — Martello tower; Stephen, Buck Mulligan, Haines. Breakfast tension; grief over mother's death; resentment of Mulligan; Stephen decides not to return.
   - Side note: Buck in *Portrait* vs. here (comparison thread)
2. **Nestor** — Stephen teaches history; wages from Mr. Deasy; Deasy's lecture on England, money, Jews. Stephen alienated, directionless.
3. **Proteus** — Sandymount Strand walk; perception, memory, identity; pure stream of consciousness.

### Part II — The Odyssey (4–15)
*Bloom's day: wandering, desire, humiliation, Dublin's social world*

4. **Calypso** — Bloom cooks kidney, feeds cat, brings Molly her mail — including Boylan's letter; senses the afternoon affair.
5. **Lotus Eaters** — Martha Clifford's letter; Catholic service; the bath.
6. **Hades** — Carriage to Dignam's funeral; death, father's suicide, dead son Rudy; spots Boylan.
7. **Aeolus** — Newspaper offices; mock-headline fragments; Bloom's ad; rhetoric and jokes.
8. **Lestrygonians** — Hungry wandering; disgust at animalistic eating; cheese sandwich at Davy Byrne's.
9. **Scylla and Charybdis** — National Library; Stephen's Shakespeare/Hamlet theory; literary sparring.
10. **Wandering Rocks** — 19 vignettes; the city as organism; Bloom intermittent; Dublin's social web.
11. **Sirens** — Ormond Hotel bar; written in musical motifs; flirtation, music, Boylan en route to Molly; desire and jealousy.
12. **Cyclops** — Barney Kiernan's pub; the Citizen's anti-Semitic rage; Bloom's moral universalism; parody of epic bluster.
    - Key beat: Bloom on force vs. love — *"Force, hatred, history... Love, the opposite of hatred"* (12.1481-5)
    - Bloom names Jewish philosophers/artists, Jesus chief among them → Citizen hurls the biscuit tin as Bloom's carriage departs.
13. **Nausicaa** — Sandymount Strand; Gerty MacDowell; fireworks; parody of sentimental romance prose.
14. **Oxen of the Sun** — Maternity hospital; drunken medical students; prose evolves through the entire history of English style, Latin → slang.
15. **Circe** — Nighttown; hallucinatory dream-play; fantasy, guilt, sexuality, humiliation.

### Part III — The Nostos (16–18)
*Return, reconciliation, home*

16. **Eumaeus** — Cabman's shelter; sailors and pseudo-experts; Bloom's paternal role toward Stephen.
17. **Ithaca** — Catechism Q&A; astronomy, religion, identity; Stephen departs; Bloom urinates under the stars; domestic order.
18. **Penelope** — Molly's unpunctuated eight-sentence monologue; marriage, sexuality, memory, the first "yes"; final "yes."

## III. Close Readings by Episode

### A. Calypso / Lotus Eaters (4–5): Letters and the Bath
1. **Milly's letter** — "new tam... young student... Blazes Boylan's seaside girls"; her 15th birthday; separation anxiety; "Molly. Milly. Same thing watered down."
   - Bloom's troubled affection: "A wild piece of goods... Destiny. Ripening now. Vain: very."
2. **Boylan's letter to Molly** — "Bold hand. Marion." — the 4 p.m. program; *La ci darem* + *Love's Old Sweet Song*.
3. **Metempsychosis** — "met him pike hoses"; reincarnation motif introduced over burnt kidney.
4. **Martha Clifford's letter** — "naughty boy... I will punish you"; the pen-pal affair as mediated, distant eroticism; "what kind of perfume does your wife use."
5. **The bath foreseen** — "This is my body"; womb of warmth; "the limp father of thousands, a languid floating flower." Bloom's body as passive, floating, feminized.

### B. Hades (6): Death Thread
1. Carriage with Cunningham, Power, Dedalus.
2. Rudy — "My son inside her. I could have helped him on in life."
3. Father's suicide; Dignam's death; the man in the brown mackintosh (recurring mystery figure).

### C. Wandering Rocks (10): City Choreography
1. Boylan waylays Jack Mooney's brother-in-law outside la Maison Claire ("humpy, tight, making for the liberties").
2. Cunningham–Power–Wyse Nolan charity walk for the Dignams; the assistant town clerk's corns ("shower of hail suit," hasty steps past Micky Anderson's watches).
3. Technique: cinematic quick cuts; micro-portraits; Boylan sprinkled through the city as a shadow over Bloom's day.

### D. Sirens (11): The Musical Episode — deep dive
1. **The overture** — "Bronze by gold heard the hoofirons, steelyringing / Imperthnthn thnthnthn"
   - Bronze = Miss Douce; Gold = Miss Kennedy; hair color as timbre
   - Boylan motif = hoofbeats/jingle; Lenehan's "chips" as percussion
   - "Blew. Blue bloom is on the. / Goldpinnacled hair" — pun chain blew→blue→Bloom; melodic anticipation and resolution
2. **The street-cry fugue** ("Molly in her shift in Lombard street west...")
   - Erotic memory → catalogue of trade sounds (hunter's horn, shepherd's pipe, policeman's whistle, towncrier)
   - "Sonnez la cloche" — Miss Douce's garter-snap motif
   - "All is lost now" — *La Sonnambula* motif
   - "Pom" drumbeats; "da capo" — Bloom recognizes the musical structure of his own thoughts
   - Dignam funeral motif intrudes ("Poor little nominedomine")
   - The whore of the lane — abrupt modulation funeral → erotic; "Knew Molly" as final chord
3. **The catgut passage** ("Thou lost one. All songs on that theme.")
   - The human voice: "two tiny silky chords"
   - Silence-as-sound: "It's in the silence after you feel you hear. Vibrations."
   - Bloom's plucked rubber band/catgut = private counter-melody, the anti-aria
   - Polyphony via "while... while... while" scoring
   - Sirens emotional arc: seduction → attachment → rupture → lament
   - Grotesque counterpoint: "rat's tail wriggling," "belly like a poisoned pup"
   - Wave-language: "wavyavyeavyheavyeavyevyevyhair un comb:'d"
4. **M'appari scene** — Simon Dedalus sings (*Martha*); Cowley accompanies; Richie Goulding on Joe Maas; "Last Farewell" imagery (headland, ship, waving girl)
5. **Pub-talk about Molly** — "Mrs Marion Bloom has left off clothes of all descriptions"; Marion Tweedy; "daughter of the regiment"; Gibraltar; "My Irish Molly, O" — Molly as Dublin's collective erotic myth vs. Bloom's private tenderness
6. Garter smack scene — Boylan, Lenehan, Miss Douce: "You're the essence of vulgarity."

### E. Cyclops (12): Politics and the Citizen
1. Narration split: the Nameless One + parodic interpolations.
2. Mock-epic inflation: "O'Bloom, the son of Rory... he of the prudent soul."
3. The Citizen on the *Irish Independent*; nationalism as bluster.
4. Bloom's stand: Jewish identity + Irish nationality; persecution; the love speech.
5. Sports debate (lawn tennis, hurley); Bloom's "rower's heart" lecturing — "he'd talk about it for an hour."
6. The James Wought / Zaretsky swindle anecdote (emigration scam).
7. Exit: biscuit-tin hurled; Elijah-ascension parody.

### F. Nausicaa (13): Gerty
1. Gerty's introduction — narrated in Princess Novelette style: "as fair a specimen of winsome Irish girlhood"; iron jelloids, eyebrowleine, "Thursday for wealth."
2. Her fantasy husband — tall, broad shoulders, trimmed moustache, "brekky," the continental honeymoon.
3. The Roman candle scene — leaning back, "wondrous revealment," mutual complicity ("she let him and she saw that he saw").
4. Bloom after — "My fireworks. Up like a rocket, down like a stick"; the day's inventory ("Long day I've had. Martha, the bath, funeral, house of Keyes, museum...").
5. **Portrait parallel** — Gerty vs. the birdgirl (Ch. IV):
   - Both beach encounters; both women blush and permit the gaze
   - Shared diction: "ivorylike purity," blue clothing, "hither, thither," skirts bared
   - Joyce ironizes both Stephen and his own younger authorial self; contrast between the two protagonists' responses.
6. Publication history — July–Aug 1920 *Little Review* seizure; NY Society for the Suppression of Vice; obscenity trial → ban in English-speaking countries (Groden 169).

### G. Oxen of the Sun (14)
1. Style-history technique; "time's ruins build eternity's mansions... In woman's womb word is made flesh."
2. Late-chapter slang cacophony: "Golly, whatten tunket's yon guy in the mackintosh?"; Bartle the Bread; Alexander J. Christ Dowie's revival-preacher blast.
3. Joyce spent ~1,000 hours writing it.

### H. Circe (15): The Hallucination Play — deep dive
1. **Composition** — begun spring 1920; expected 2–3 months, took 8 drafts / 6+ months; forced revision of the whole novel; ~1/3 of Ulysses written at proof stage (Beach 58). Karen Lawrence: "a stage for a libidinous release of tendencies in the language and in the characters" (150-51).
2. **Opening** — Mabbot street entrance of Nighttown; skeleton tracks, will-o'-the-wisps; Cissy Caffrey's song; Privates Carr and Compton; the deafmute idiot; the Bawd; Edy Boardman's bickering.
3. **Bloom's entrance sequence**
   - Esperanto / "Gaelic league spy" mutter
   - "In Darkest Stepaside" — the Irish Cyclist letter, civic pride, signpost
   - Caffrey twins collision; "weak hams"; pocket-check (watch, purse, *Sweets of Sin*, potato, soap) — pockets as inventory of Bloom's soul
   - Pickpocket paranoia: "Old thieves' dodge. Collide. Then snatch your purse."
   - Ellen Bloom apparition (pantomime dame); "Poldy!"
4. **The trial fantasies**
   - The Crier's indictment: "dynamitard, forger, bigamist, bawd and cuckold"
   - Recorder Falkiner with Mosaic ramshorns
   - Mrs Yelverton Barry, Mrs Bellingham ("Venus in furs" letters), the Hon. Mrs Mervyn Talboys with the hunting crop — masochistic flogging fantasy
   - The erotic photocard testimony (señorita/torero)
   - The cuckoo timepiece: "Cuckoo. Cuckoo. Cuckoo." + jingling bed quoits
   - Jury of the day's men; the Nameless One
   - Paddy Dignam's ghost (beagle → corpse): "List, list, O list!" — Hamlet ghost parody; comic/horrific vindication
   - Hynes denies knowing him: "You are a perfect stranger."
5. **Metamorphoses of Bloom**
   - Litany of the Daughters of Erin: "Wandering Soap... Sweets of Sin... Potato Preservative, pray for us"
   - Mulligan's medical testimony: "bisexually abnormal... prematurely bald"
   - Pregnancy/birth fantasy: "O, I so want to be a mother" — eight metallic octuplets
   - Zoe and the infantilization scene: babylinen, "One two tlee," the talking buckles ("Love me. Love me not."), the male brutes — humiliation as absolution (a baby cannot be blamed)
   - Gerty's apparition: "You did that. I hate you. / I love you for doing that to me."
   - Mrs Breen: "Mr Bloom! You down here in the haunts of sin!"
   - Shoe-fitting / foot fetish: Manfield's, "sweet buttonhooking," the Hoof: "Smell my hot goathide."
   - Shakespeare in the mirror, "rigid in facial paralysis," crowned by antlers (cuckold horns): "Iagogo!"
   - Boylan apparition: tosses sixpence, hangs hat on Bloom's antlered head — "I have a little private business with your wife" / Bloom: "Thank you, sir. Yes, sir."
6. **The Bohee brothers scene** (minstrelsy)
   - Molly's "slumming" fantasy; "Othello black brute"; Eugene Stratton; Livermore christies
   - Tom & Sam Bohee (real 1880s–90s Black banjoist brothers) rendered in grotesque minstrel caricature — the subconscious remixing racist popular culture under pressure of guilt/jealousy
   - "There's someone in the house with Dina" → the someone in Bloom's house is Boylan
7. **Ending** — Bloom's Masonic murmurs; the apparition of **Rudy** at 11 (Eton suit, glass shoes, bronze helmet, reading right to left, kissing the page); "BLOOM: (Wonderstruck, calls inaudibly) Rudy!"

### I. Eumaeus / Ithaca (16–17)
1. Eumaeus opening: Bloom brushes shavings off Stephen "in orthodox Samaritan fashion."
2. Ithaca's drawer inventory — the erotic photocards (Box 32, Charing Cross), magnifying glass, Hungarian lottery coupons
   - Reading: not deviance but character — voyeurism, curiosity, mediated connection, female agency / male passivity themes; parallels Martha correspondence
3. Bloom's four-stage response to the bed's evidence: **envy → jealousy → abnegation → equanimity**; betrayal rationalized as natural fact.

## IV. Thematic Threads

### A. The Bloom–Boylan–Molly Triangle (the novel's engine)
1. The affair is happening *today*, June 16, 1904, 4 p.m.; triangular awareness (all three know).
2. Boylan as anti-Bloom foil:
   - Bloom: introspective, gentle, cerebral eroticism, empathetic
   - Boylan: physical, impulsive, swaggering, self-satisfied
3. The 4 p.m. appointment as narrative clock — posters, mentions, the jingle, the carriage ride as "suspenseful non-event."
4. Boylan as psychic presence — phantom rival, projection of insecurity and fantasy.
5. Circe: Boylan as hallucinated tormentor; humiliation and eroticism fuse.
6. Penelope reversal: Boylan deflated (crude, less than imagined); Molly prefers Bloom emotionally/intellectually. Boylan = catalyst, not replacement.

### B. Cuckoldry, Masochism, and Equanimity
1. Does Bloom "enjoy" it? Not simply — masochism + resignation + voyeurism.
2. Circe hallucinations: Bloom as servant/voyeur to the affair — mastering pain by leaning into it.
3. The "scientific defense": envy/jealousy/abnegation/equanimity — betrayal as tide or gravity.
4. Vicarious connection: Boylan as proxy for the vitality Bloom stopped providing after Rudy's death.
5. Power-shift reading: acceptance as survival — humiliation over desolation; contrast with Othello's rage.
6. Motifs: cuckoo clock (cuckoo→cuckold), antlers/horns, "someone in the house with Dina," Davy Stephens's "addresses of all the cuckolds in Dublin."

### C. Rudy and Lost Fatherhood
1. Dead at 11 days, eleven years ago; no full marital sexual relationship since.
2. "My son inside her. I could have helped him on in life."
3. Bloom's paternal drift toward Stephen (Eumaeus/Ithaca).
4. The Circe finale apparition as the day's emotional resolution.
5. Caffrey children as reminders of lost fatherhood.

### D. Music as Structure (Sirens technique, spreading beyond ep. 11)
1. Onomatopoeia as notation ("pom," "pwee," "heehaw," "Imperthnthn").
2. Motif, echo, vibration, silence-as-sound, da capo.
3. Polyphonic "while" scoring; characters as instruments (bronze/gold).
4. Sirens idiom recurs in Bloom's monologue even inside other episodes.
5. Beauty ↔ grotesque oscillation as musical dynamic.

### E. Antisemitism and Irish Nationalism
1. Haines (ep. 1): "I don't want to see my country fall into the hands of German jews... our national problem" (p. 37).
2. Deasy (ep. 2): "England is in the hands of the jews... signs of a nation's decay... Old England is dying" + the harlot's cry couplet (Blake).
3. The Citizen (ep. 12): open rage; the biscuit tin.
4. Bloom's counter: love speech; naming Jewish thinkers; dual identity (Jewish + Irish).
5. Kosher/Yom Kippur passing references in Bloom's monologue.

### F. Race and Minstrelsy
1. The Bohee brothers hallucination; Othello; Eugene Stratton.
2. Joyce deploying period racist caricature to expose the subconscious, not endorse it — grotesque distortion under guilt/jealousy pressure.

### G. Women's Voices and the Gaze
1. Gerty's chapter-length interior style → foreshadows Molly's Penelope voice.
2. The birdgirl / Gerty doubling across *Portrait* and *Ulysses*.
3. Molly as public myth (pub talk) vs. private person (Penelope).
4. "Cuckqueen" note (~26:30) — the inverse-gender concept flagged.

### H. Stephen Threads
1. Mother-grief and guilt; "Ferguson" (Yeats's *Who Goes with Fergus?*) at Circe's end.
2. Shakespeare theory: Hamlet as autobiography; "Hamlet, I am thy father's spirit" echoes.
3. Nietzsche: Übermensch, Zarathustra (Mulligan's mock-heroics, ~45:45–46:30).
4. Kevin Egan in Paris (Proteus memory, p. 76): gunpowder cigarettes, absinthe ("green fairy"), Fenian exile.
5. Proteus imagery log: the dog (147:30–149), "hind paws," cocklepickers, "touch me... lonely," lagoons of sand rising/flowing (p. 89), "As I am. All or not at all."

### I. The Body, Food, Bathing
1. The bath: "This is my body" — Eucharist parody + bodily tenderness.
2. Lestrygonians food disgust; "Hermit with a platter of pulse."
3. Kidney, pork, kosher tension; "pork steaks."
4. Loose physical comedy: flatulence "cure" (449:45), "limp spine," Bloom's jaundice remark.
